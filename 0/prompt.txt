Claude's conclusion is appropriately conservative. The investigation has now established something more important than whether 75°C is "right": **the existing thermal policy has no demonstrated engineering basis.**

The evidence separates into three categories. The 90°C benchmark abort is historical configuration, not a qualified limit. The kernel/device readings provide actual sensor-specific critical/emergency values, but those are protection limits rather than necessarily desirable operating thresholds. And the live tests establish observed behavior under the present llama.cpp workload: approximately 55/58°C package/VRAM on the lead GPU, 66/72°C on the 27B GPU during the >100-second test, with an earlier 78°C VRAM observation and no thermal throttling. The observed throttling was PL2 power limiting, not thermal limiting.

That makes Claude's refusal to change thresholds correct. In particular, I agree with rejecting my earlier suggestion that something like 85/95 might be reasonable. We now have better evidence, and there is no basis yet for selecting those numbers either.

### I would authorize the 30–60 minute soak

This is a case where additional investigation is justified because it resolves a concrete engineering question. The short test hasn't established thermal equilibrium. The important measurement isn't another instantaneous peak, but the **temperature curve and eventual plateau under sustained representative load**.

I would make the experiment slightly more rigorous than simply "run both GPUs for an hour." Have Claude preserve per-sensor time series for package, VRAM channels, memory controller and PCIe, along with GPU utilization, frequency, power, PL1/PL2 state, thermal-throttle flags, fan data if exposed, inference throughput, and ambient/intake temperature if the host exposes anything credible. Sampling at one second is appropriate given the current abort mechanism.

The workload should be stable enough to distinguish warm-up from steady state. The analysis should identify whether each relevant sensor reaches an apparent plateau, its peak, sustained range near the end, and whether throughput/frequency changes correlate with thermal or power throttling. **Do not intentionally drive the hardware toward the reported critical limits.** This is normal sustained inference characterization, not thermal stress-to-failure testing.

I would also have it evaluate the current 90°C abort behavior separately. The finding that **one sample from any sensor trips the entire benchmark** deserves examination independent of the numeric threshold. A single noisy VRAM-channel reading probably should not automatically have identical semantics to sustained package overheating. Sensor-specific thresholds plus a short persistence/debounce policy may eventually be better, but the soak should gather evidence before designing that.

### One thing I would add before setting final thresholds

Claude should look up Intel's published B65 thermal documentation now. Not because the device-reported limits are invalid, but because we're about to turn measurements into operational policy. That's exactly when external authoritative evidence has high information value.

The resulting evidence hierarchy would be strong:

**Intel specification + device-reported protection limits + actual sustained behavior in this exact T5820 + operational margin → monitoring/abort policy.**

If Intel doesn't publish sufficiently specific VRAM/package guidance, record that as unresolved rather than deriving a magic number.

There's also a useful discovery in the power data. GPU1 spent 60/90 samples under PL2 limiting while losing only about 3% clock. That's not necessarily a problem, but it's a real characteristic of this configuration. I wouldn't optimize it now. Preserve it as an observation so future performance/power tuning has a baseline.

And Claude followed the scope instruction correctly: streaming, permanent Prometheus infrastructure, and queue enforcement were left alone; only the stale observability documentation was corrected. That's the right stopping discipline.

So my authorization would be: **proceed with the bounded sustained thermal soak and Intel specification research; make no threshold or guardrail changes during data collection.** Afterward, we review the measurements and decide separately on package warning/critical, VRAM warning/critical, benchmark abort threshold, and persistence/debounce semantics. That would turn the current placeholders into evidence-backed operating policy rather than simply replacing one set of guesses with another.
