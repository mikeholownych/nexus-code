This is a much stronger result than the point observations suggested, because Claude now has enough longitudinal data to distinguish transient values from sustained behavior.

The most important result is not the peak temperatures. It is the combination of **thermal equilibrium + stable throughput + zero thermal-throttle indications**. Both 22-minute loaded phases apparently reached equilibrium quickly, with 90% of the temperature rise occurring within 83–189 seconds and last-five-minute slopes within ±0.14 °C/min. If those calculations reproduce from the raw samples, that is strong evidence that we were observing steady-state behavior within this tested envelope, rather than temperatures that were still climbing when the experiment ended.

The actual operating points are also useful. Decode steady state was about **66°C package / 76°C hottest VRAM on GPU0**, and **69°C package / 82.6°C hottest VRAM on GPU1**, with GPU1 peaking at 84°C VRAM. Prefill ran several degrees cooler despite the enormous prompt throughput we saw. Most importantly, Claude reports **zero thermal, prochot, VR thermal, and RATL flags across all 3,244 samples on both GPUs**, while throughput remained essentially flat across each phase.

That materially strengthens the proposition:

> **Within this workload, ambient condition, hardware configuration and observation period, the T5820 cooling system reached a thermal equilibrium while both GPUs sustained useful inference without observed thermal throttling or temperature-correlated throughput degradation.**

That's a defensible conclusion. It says nothing about warmer rooms, restricted airflow, different workloads, component aging, or maximum safe temperatures.

### The surprising result is GPU1's PL2 behavior

GPU1 apparently asserted PL2 power limiting for **94% of loaded samples**, while GPU0 never did. Yet GPU1's frequency remained around 2333 MHz during decode versus 2400 MHz nominal, only about 2.8% lower, and throughput remained stable.

That's potentially much more interesting for future optimization than thermals.

It doesn't yet establish that increasing or changing power limits would improve useful performance. It establishes that **the 27B workload is routinely encountering a power-control boundary before encountering a thermal-control boundary** under this test.

That's exactly the kind of thing this experiment was supposed to uncover. We started asking a thermal question and discovered another potentially relevant operating constraint. No reason to tune it yet. Preserve it as the next hypothesis.

### The current 75°C VRAM warning has now been experimentally characterized

This is particularly clear.

During loaded operation, VRAM was at or above 75°C for **39% of GPU0's loaded samples and 95.5% of GPU1's**. Yet there was no corresponding thermal throttling, throughput degradation, failure, or inability to reach equilibrium.

So Claude's wording that the existing warning largely means *“inference is running”* is directionally fair for this workload.

That doesn't prove 75°C is universally safe or tell us what the replacement threshold should be. It demonstrates that **75°C is poorly discriminating as an operational warning for this qualified workload**.

And Claude correctly did **not** change it.

### The abort analysis uncovered a design issue separate from temperature selection

The current benchmark safety mechanism appears to use the hottest reading from all temperature sensors and abort on a single sample.

The soak suggests that sensor selection may actually be reasonable in one respect: the maximum VRAM reading tracks the hottest physical GDDR channel, which is meaningfully hotter than the median. We shouldn't average that away.

But **single-sample threshold semantics** are a separate question.

At an 80°C threshold, GPU1 would apparently have generated 47 aborts with no persistence requirement, despite only 20 excursions lasting ≥30 seconds and one lasting ≥60 seconds. At 85°C or 90°C, none occurred.

So there are really at least three independent policy decisions here:

```text
WHAT is measured?
    package / hottest VRAM / other sensors

WHAT does the threshold mean?
    warning / critical / protective abort

HOW must it occur?
    instantaneous / persistence / hysteresis / combination
```

Those shouldn't be collapsed into one temperature number.

### Intel documentation remains appropriately unresolved

I'm glad Claude didn't manufacture a B65 limit from neighboring products or reinterpret the unexplained `temp2_max=60°C`.

The useful facts remain device-reported protection points and Linux driver semantics. The soak establishes observed behavior below those protection points. Neither establishes Intel's unpublished recommended continuous operating temperature.

So **“maximum safe B65 temperature” remains unknown**, and it doesn't prevent us from establishing our own conservative operational policy from measured behavior.

### There are also two secondary findings worth preserving

The busiest CPU core reaching **98% while total CPU was only 28%** supports the existing observation that a single-thread bottleneck remains somewhere in the llama.cpp path. That's now worth keeping on the future optimization list, but not disturbing the current system over.

And the workload design was better than simply “54 minutes at full load”: approximately 22 minutes decode-heavy followed by 22 minutes of unique ~14K-token prompts exercised two materially different inference regimes. That helps explain why we saw very different KV/cache and prompt/decode behavior while watching the TUI.

Your auditory observation belongs in the evidence package too, but exactly as we just scoped it: **no conspicuous fan-noise excursion was perceived by the operator in a noisy multi-host environment**. It shouldn't be used to infer RPM or cooling-controller behavior.

Overall, I would accept this soak as **strong evidence for thermal adequacy within the tested operating envelope**, subject to reproducing Claude's calculations from the preserved raw evidence before treating the numerical conclusions as independently corroborated.

And I would leave every threshold unchanged for the moment.

The experiment has done something better than giving us an arbitrary new set of numbers. It has given us enough evidence to now design the **temperature policy deliberately**: separate package from VRAM, separate warning from protective action, decide whether persistence/hysteresis belongs in each, and determine the operational margins we actually want.

That should be a small follow-on analysis, not another blind soak.
